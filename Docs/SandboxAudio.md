# Sandbox audio and visual feedback

Sandbox and campaign controllers load `Assets/MetaHackthone/Resources/SandboxSoundBank.asset` automatically. A level can override it through `DownhillSettings.SoundBank`. Existing scenes do not need to be regenerated or saved.

The bank has 26 cues and 62 mono, 48 kHz clips. Grabbing a part plays a sound for its type (`SandboxPresentation.GrabCue`): wooden blocks and the core use `Grab`, wheels use `GrabWheel` (rubber ball grabs), and the drill uses `GrabDrill` (an unpowered drill handled). When the drill breaks a drop-bridge target, `SandboxPuzzleRun` plays `DrillBreak` (a stone slab cracking) once at the contact. New cues are appended after `Water` because the bank stores cues by number; `SandboxSoundBankTests` lists the loop cues (Rolling to Water) explicitly. **Since 2026-11-16 every clip is a recorded Soundsnap Pro+ library sound** (Soundsnap license), replacing the earlier procedural starter sounds. Construction cues and impacts are natural foley (wooden toy blocks, wooden box pops, rubber ball bounces, small metal clanks, ratchet clicks); loops are a toy car on wood, a light wooden cart, a Dremel, and a small stream; countdown/result/reward/transfer cues are designed game stingers and magic effects. Each clip's source file and hash are in `Assets/MetaHackthone/Audio/Sandbox/SourceManifest.json`.

Raw downloads live outside `Assets` in `MetaHackthone/sfx/` (about 51 MB, not imported by Unity). The old procedural clips are backed up in `sfx/_original_synth/`. `sfx/_tools/conform_sfx.py` (numpy + scipy) rebuilds the bank clips from the raw files: mono 48 kHz PCM16, 0.6 peak, 25 Hz high-pass, silent one-shot edges, and equal-power-crossfaded loops taken from the steadiest window; `sfx/_tools/check_clips.py` checks the test rules. Notes: `Button_3` is a genuinely 16 ms tap; `Reject_3` is a pitched-down copy because the two wood-drop sources only contain three usable hits.

## Tuning

Select the sound bank to replace clips or adjust cue volume, pitch variation, cooldown, voice limit, spatial blend, and distance. Frequent interactions and material impacts each have four variations and avoid immediate repetition. The existing project Meta XR Audio spatializer is enabled on fully spatial sources. Countdown, go, and result cues retain a small positional component while staying clearly audible.

`Assets/MetaHackthone/Audio/Sandbox/Sandbox.mixer` exposes Interactions, Vehicle, Ambience, and Rewards groups. Master attenuation starts at -6 dB. Source levels leave headroom; individual gameplay cues remain louder than ambience. Reward presentation reduces ambience to 30% through the bank setting. No music was added.

Optional `SandboxAudioSurface` components on a collider or its parent select wood, rubber, or metal impact textures. Without an override, wheel impacts use rubber, drill impacts use metal, and other contacts use wood. No collider, physical material, socket, or saved-construction changes are needed.

## Event behavior

- `SandboxPresentation.Presented` delivers gameplay feedback to audio and VFX independently, with cue, world contact position/normal, intensity, material, source, and actual part size. `AssemblyWorld.InteractionPresented` supplies confirmed construction events; the original `Interaction` event remains available. Attachment occurs at joint commit and detachment uses the separated socket. Raw connection restoration/removal and cancelled alignments remain silent. `CuePlayed` remains an audio diagnostic, not a VFX trigger.
- Vehicle probes use actual wheel contacts. Rolling volume and pitch follow supported translational speed; airborne or stationary wheels fade silent. Normal collision speed controls impact weight. Low-speed contacts, within-vehicle collisions, and repeated contact bursts are suppressed.
- The active drill's visual spin drives the drill loop. Water-failure detection in the 2.5D and 3D runners plays a splash. Water-shader scenery supplies a localized ambient source; the largest active water renderer is selected once per course root.
- Launch gathering and traveling cues follow the existing transfer timing. Arrival plays when the transfer finishes. Campaign launches preserve the existing short pause without adding a 3-2-1; other countdowns tick on number changes. Go occurs when physics starts.
- Success, reward reveal, and reward claim use distinct cues. Victory return starts after the existing victory pause. Retry/return clear vehicle contacts and transient sounds. Build ambience can resume after reset; pause, disabled controllers, and scene destruction stop playback.
- Runtime audio uses a fixed pool of 12 one-shot sources plus five dedicated sources. Audio variation uses a separate random generator and does not affect gameplay randomness.

## Coordinated VFX

`DownhillSettings.Vfx` exposes the enabled switch, palette, timings, part-relative sizes, impact threshold, and transient limits. Controllers create this presentation at runtime; authored scenes need no changes. Grab briefly brightens the existing outline (0.12s); attachment expands a socket-aligned gold ring with eight motes (0.25s); detachment contracts a ring; a rejected attempt pulses the existing red ghost (0.18s). No new pulse occurs just from hovering a blocked placement.

Impacts create a few wood, rubber, or metal-colored flecks at the actual contact. Quiet contacts and ordinary rolling produce no bursts. Valid drill contact creates small sandstone chips with an 80ms cooldown; the existing physical bridge-break debris remains responsible for the breaking hit. Water failures use pooled droplets and two surface-aligned ripples at the swept contact point in both runners. Since 2026-11-16 campaign factory levels also treat their water as a hazard: `DownhillFactory.CheckFactoryWater` reads the flat water-shader meshes under the `SandboxFactoryLevel` (waterfall curtains, droplets, and spring runs are excluded by flatness) and fails the run with a Splash, droplets, and ripples as soon as a vehicle collider's lowest point drops below a surface inside that mesh's footprint. No colliders or prefab edits are needed; new water must use a readable, lying mesh with a shader whose name contains "water". Splash may take a voice from a vehicle impact when the pool is full, and its volume was raised to 0.45 so it reads over the Failure sting. Standalone courses without a level presentation retain their legacy visual fallback.

Launch/victory transfer keeps the existing gold fragments, with a compact ring on actual arrival. Success adds a short gold accent. Reward reveal adds six motes, cancellation releases them, and claim adds one contracting pulse that can finish after the old reward coordinator is destroyed.

Transient VFX are limited to 64 particles and eight pooled rings. Particles share the existing **512 total** fragment budget with transfer/reward effects; priority allocations reclaim minor bursts immediately, so the transient pool never reserves capacity from a transfer. All particle and ring renderers use the shared `Resources/SandboxFeedback.mat` and the existing stereo-capable, depth-tested `SandboxBoundary` shader. Effects create no colliders, rigidbodies, lights, camera motion, or saved construction data. Pause, retry, return, controller disable, and scene destruction clear transient state. Audio mute, missing clips, and exhausted audio voices do not gate visuals.

For headset review, compare `Vfx.Enabled` on/off at the same refresh rate and check socket ring scale, hand/part occlusion in both eyes, clack-to-ring timing, drill contact, and water entry. Profile repeated attachment plus launch/reward transitions; editor tests do not establish headset frame time or stereo comfort.

## Rebuilding audio and verification

**Do not run `Tools/Build-SandboxAudio.py` now**: it overwrites the recorded clips with the old procedural ones. To rebuild recorded clips, run `python -I sfx/_tools/conform_sfx.py sfx Assets/MetaHackthone/Audio/Sandbox` (with numpy and scipy installed), then refresh Unity. Historically, `python Tools/Build-SandboxAudio.py` regenerated the starter WAVs, then **Tools > Sandbox audio > Build sound bank** to import them and create missing bank/mixer entries. Rebuilding preserves existing bank tuning and clip replacements. Back up customized WAVs before regenerating them.

Automated validation covers clip completeness, routing, waveform headroom, loop boundaries, attachment commit/cancellation, silent save restoration, voice limits, sample variation, pause/disable, launch/retry/return, and physical wheel support versus airborne silence. Existing attachment, victory, and reward regression tests are also run.
