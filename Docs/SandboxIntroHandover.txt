SANDBOX FIRST-ENTRY INTRO - HANDOVER
Rewritten: 2026-11-15, America/New_York

WHAT IT IS
The first time a player enters Level 1 in the campaign, a ~42 second intro plays
before they get control. It replaced the earlier "Almost" intro (a Level 5 stage
with four cars, authored in Blender), which was deleted from the Unity project on
2026-11-15 at the user's request. Nothing here is authored in Blender and there
is no baked clip: the whole intro is runtime code on the real Level 1.

  1. Darken       The level and the kit are hidden; the room fades to 12 %.
  2. Orb          A blue spark with a soft fog halo makes one and a half
                  shrinking loops across the player's view and settles where the
                  building core sits on the bench. It never leaves the view cone.
                  Twelve parts hang adrift in the dark along its way: they can
                  only be seen where the spark's light reaches them, and the
                  ones it flies straight through are pushed aside by its wake.
  3. Core birth   The core swells out of the spark (0 -> 1.45 -> 0.9 -> 1), the
                  fog draws in and clears, a blue glow stays on the core.
  4. First car    The pull: space twists shut around the core like a black hole
                  (see THE PULL below) and stays open while it builds.
                  A short block, a long block and two wheels come out of the
                  dark BEHIND the core, unseen until its light reaches them,
                  and snap on one per beat with the game's attach feedback.
                  The result makes no sense: a mast, a bar sticking out
                  sideways, a wheel lying on the bar, a wheel for a nose.
  5. Launch       The track brightens out of darkness, a beacon marks its start,
                  and the car is sent there with the game's launch mote effect.
  6. First run    Real physics, untouched. It topples, slides about a metre
                  down the bridge and comes to rest by itself (about 4 s).
  7. Return       The core lets go. Nothing is pushed: the parts stay where the
                  car stopped and dissolve. The core returns to the start as
                  blue motes.
  8. Second car   The pull again, at the start. Nearly the starter car: the
                  long block ahead of the core and two wheels, but both wheels
                  on the same side (the viewer's), front and back.
  9. Second run   Real physics, untouched. It rolls on its wheeled side, drags
                  on the other, drifts across the bridge and drops off the far
                  edge about two thirds of the way down (about 2 s). The run
                  ends when it leaves the level's test bounds, under the water.
 10. Home         The core flies back to the player; the building area appears
                  and the room returns to normal.
 11. Starter car  One last pull: the level's own starter car (long block and two
                  wheels) assembles on the core; the spare parts settle.
 12. Turn         The rotation disc whirls the build through two full turns in
                  1.8 s, spinning up and settling, in the game's 5-degree steps,
                  and ends exactly where it began.
 13. Title        "Your turn" (SandboxFirstEntryIntro.TitleText), then control.

THE PULL (user requirement, 2026-11-15: "space distortion ... like a black hole")
When the core gathers parts, a disc of space around it is twisted: what lies
behind is swirled about the core and bent as light is bent past a heavy mass,
with a black well in the middle, a thin ring of trapped light and faint matter
spiralling in. It replaced three blue rings closing in on the core, which the
user did not like. It opens in 0.6 s, swells on every build beat and shuts just
after the last part lands. Parts in flight are smeared around the well; the core
and every part that has landed are drawn untouched in front of it.

SIZE (user direction, 2026-11-15, drawn on a still of the pull at the track).
The first version was too large. The user marked three circles around the core:
  red     the black area                 0.133 m   1.9 part widths
  yellow  out to here, the twist         0.220 m   3.2 part widths
  green   out to here, less twist        0.383 m   5.5 part widths
(16, 26.5 and 46.2 px on the still, at 120.7 px a metre; a part is 0.0698 m wide
at the track.) These are PullBlackUnits, PullTwistUnits and PullUnits in
SandboxFirstEntryIntro, in part widths, so the pull is the same size against
the build beside the player, where a part is 0.0349 m wide: twist to 11 cm,
disc 19 cm. Beyond the twist proper only _LensWeak (0.1) of the twist is left,
fading to nothing at the rim.
The black area beside the player is the exception. At 1.9 part widths (6.6 cm)
the user still found it too big on screen and drew a second red circle, on a
still of the last build: 78 px where the black area had been 139 px. So there
it is NearPullBlackUnits, 1.07 part widths (3.7 cm): it only just clears the
core. The twist and the disc beside the player were not changed.
An edit-mode test pins all of these numbers.

MOTION (user direction, 2026-11-15). The black hole has to keep playing until it
disappears. The first version opened, then stood almost still (only a small
swell on each beat and faint moving arms) until it shut. Now what is seen in the
twist flows around the black area for as long as the lens is open, faster the
nearer it is: _LensFlow (2.4 rad a cycle at the black area) and _LensFlowRate
(0.45 cycles a second) on the shader. It is done by cross-fading two copies of
the picture half a cycle apart, so the flow never winds the picture up; that
costs a second texture read for each pixel of the disc.

THE PULL IS NOW A BLUEPRINT AND TETHERS (user direction, 2026-11-16). This
replaces everything about the black hole below (the lens, its sizes, motion,
the part stretch). The user found the black hole made the whole level go dark
on the headset (that was the full-frame bending in the 20:46 build) and asked
for a 3D effect, not billboards, in the style of the blueprint and Ultrahand
look of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom, whose purpose is to pull each part from
the dark to the core. SandboxSpaceLens.cs and its shader were deleted.
  Blueprint   When a pull starts, a see-through copy of every part of the build
              stands at its place on the core (SandboxIntroEffects.Blueprint),
              drawn with Resources/SandboxHologram.shader: glowing edges, a
              faint body, bands of light climbing through it. It fades in over
              two beats; each copy disappears as its real part lands
              (FillSlot); whatever is left is cleared after the last landing.
  Tether      For each part, a 3D tube of light (SandboxIntroTether, mesh
              rebuilt each frame; Resources/SandboxTether.shader) reaches out
              from the core to the part in the dark over TetherReach (3/4 of a
              beat), then reels it in along its flight with pulses running
              toward the core, and is cut when it lands. Thickness in part
              widths: NearTetherUnits (0.14) beside the player, FarTetherUnits
              (0.3) at the track so it reads at 7 m.
  Grab        While held, the part carries an extra glowing-edge layer from the
              same hologram shader over its own look (Hold / Release).
Everything is light added over what is behind, so over passthrough it reads as
light in the room. Nothing is drawn over the whole view any more.
Known: in the first build the parts come from behind the core, almost along
the line of sight, so the tethers there are short on screen.

(Superseded) NO BACKGROUND DISTORTION, NO BLACK AREA (user direction, late 2026-11-15; this
supersedes SIZE and MOTION above as far as the bending and the black go). The
user did not want the pull to distort the background at all, only "the track
between the core and the part that is moving to the core", and then asked for
the black area to go too. So now:
  - SandboxSpaceLens.BendFrame is false: no frame copy, no intermediate
    texture, nothing behind the pull is bent. The disc draws only its thin
    ring of light and the faint spiral ("Plain" pass). _LensBlack on the
    shader is 0; 1 brings the black area back.
  - Each part the core pulls in is itself stretched toward the core while it
    flies: its near side is drawn out to a thin tip along the line between
    the two (SandboxIntroLit.shader, _StretchFrom/_StretchTo, driven from
    Attract() through SandboxIntroEffects.Stretch; PullStretch is how far).
    Parts in a lit scene wear the same shader with _Always (Stretchy()).
  - In the first build the parts come from behind the core, almost straight
    along the line of sight, so their stretch is foreshortened and hard to
    see there. At the track it reads clearly.
The bending code is all still there, switched off.

It is a real post-process, confined to that disc:
  Runtime/SandboxSpaceLens.cs       hands a render pass to the camera's renderer
                                    on frames in which the lens is open. The
                                    pass copies the finished frame, marks the
                                    solid parts in the stencil buffer (bit 128)
                                    and redraws the disc from the copy.
  Resources/SandboxSpaceLens.shader does the bending. One disc for both eyes,
                                    facing the head.
No renderer asset or pipeline setting was changed; the pass asks for an
intermediate texture only while the lens is open.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO: bend the real room. Passthrough is composited by the headset
underneath the frame and never reaches the app. Over passthrough the lens bends
only what is virtual (the level, parts, glows); the well is opaque black; and
where the bent image holds nothing virtual the room shows through, unbent. In
the dark first build that leaves the well, the ring, the spiral and the bent
parts and glow; at the track and in the last build the whole level swirls.

SEATED VIEW CONE (user requirement, 2026-11-15)
This is a seated experience: the player's line of sight covers about 100 degrees
without turning. Anything that flies near the player must stay inside that cone.
"Straight ahead" is the line from the player to the core. The orb, the parts the
core attracts beside the player and the starter-car parts are all placed with
InView(azimuth, elevation, distance) and kept within ViewHalfAngle (40 degrees)
either side of straight ahead, which leaves room for their glow. The first draft
flew the orb a full circle around the player; do not bring that back. The track
start sits about 40 degrees left of straight ahead in Level 1's own layout.

The tracked head is never moved. Attention is led by light, motion and positioned
sound: the beacon and the slowed mote stream lead the eye to the track (about
7 m away, down and to the left), the return stream and the brightening room lead
it back.

HOW IT WORKS
The player's kit is never touched. At the start it is copied as visual-only
"ghosts", hidden, and it is only shown again at the end of beat 11, when the
ghosts are swapped for the real parts in the same poses. Beat 12 moves the real
parts and writes their saved poses back exactly.

The two failing cars are real parts in the intro's own AssemblyWorld, built up
front above the track and kept weightless until their run. A run releases them
exactly as a test run does and uses the same miniature physics clock
(SetTestClock). After that the intro never touches a running car: no push, no
brake, no freeze. A run ends (RunCar) when the core has stayed under RestSpeed
and RestSpin for RestSeconds, or when it leaves the level's TestBounds, which
is the game's own failure rule; only then, as in the game, the car is held
where it went out so it does not fall on through the room. RunSeconds is just
a ceiling in case a car never settles. How each run ended is kept in RunEnded
and RunTime. When the core lets go (Abandon) its connections are removed and
that is all: what it leaves behind keeps whatever motion physics gives it and
dissolves 0.6 s later.

Everything the intro creates lives under one scene object, "Sandbox intro".
Restore() destroys it, restores the physics clock, room brightness, hidden kit,
level visibility and the disabled behaviours, and is safe to call at any moment
(it runs on disable and destroy). The seen key is written only after the whole
intro has played, so an interrupted intro plays again next time.

It plays only when: campaign mode, not design mode, Level 1, key unset, and the
level's starter car is built. Tests set SandboxFirstEntryIntro.Suppressed (see
SandboxIntroSuppression.cs in both play-mode test folders); without that the
intro hides the kit and breaks every other campaign fixture.

TUNING
  Timings            the Wait/Run durations inside Sequence(), beat by beat.
  Room darkness      SandboxIntroEffects.Dark (0.12).
  Orb path           OrbAt() in beat 2: loops of +/-40 degrees wide and +/-13
                     high that shrink to nothing while the orb comes in from
                     2.6 m. phase sets the number of loops (1.5), the exponent on
                     e how front-loaded the motion is.
  Final turn         TurnDegrees (720) and TurnSeconds (1.8); the easing is in
                     beat 12.
  Build tempo        BuildBeat (60/180 s): the pull's rings and every landing
                     in all three builds sit on this beat. PartFlight (0.6 s) is
                     how long a part is in the air; flights overlap.
  Parts adrift       AdriftParts (12) and AdriftScale; placed along OrbAt() in
                     beat 2, every third one directly on the path so it gets
                     pushed. Push strength and reach are in
                     SandboxIntroEffects.Drift / PushRadius.
  The light          SandboxIntroEffects.Light / LightReach / LightStrength:
                     the single point that Resources/SandboxIntroLit.shader
                     lights parts by (the spark, then the core).
  Fog                FogSize set in beats 2-3; capped at 0.7 x distance to the
                     eye in SandboxIntroEffects.Tick so it never fills the view.
  The pull           Sizes are in part widths in SandboxFirstEntryIntro:
                     PullBlackUnits (1.9, black area at the track),
                     NearPullBlackUnits (1.07, black area beside the player),
                     PullTwistUnits (3.15, end of the twist proper), PullUnits
                     (5.5, the whole disc). See SIZE above. On the shader:
                     _LensTwist (4.5 rad at the black area); _LensWeak (0.1,
                     the share of the twist left in the outer range); _LensFlow
                     and _LensFlowRate (the turning, see MOTION above);
                     _LensLight, _LensMatter. _LensWell and _LensEdge are set
                     from the sizes for each pull; their defaults are the pull
                     at the track. Opening and closing times are LensOpens and
                     LensShuts in SandboxIntroEffects; the swell on each beat
                     is in SandboxIntroEffects.Bend.
  Arrival rings      The blue rings that remain (the core's birth, the beacon at
                     the track start, the core arriving) are fx.Wave calls in
                     Sequence(); they are not part of the pull.
  Car layouts        SandboxIntroCars.Recipe. Rows are (kind, target part,
                     centre in building units, Euler rotation, target face).
  Car two's wheels   SandboxIntroCars.WheelSide. 1 (now) puts both wheels on
                     the viewer's side: they are plain to see, and the car
                     drifts away from them and drops off the FAR edge of the
                     bridge, where the bridge hides the fall. -1 mirrors it:
                     the car hides its own wheels on the way down but falls off
                     the NEAR edge into the river in full view. Both were run
                     on 2026-11-15; the paths are mirror images.
  Run endings        RestSpeed, RestSpin, RestSeconds, MinRun and the ceiling
                     RunSeconds in SandboxFirstEntryIntro.
  Colours            SandboxIntroEffects.Blue / Pale / Deep.

USER DIRECTION SO FAR (2026-11-15), in the order it was given
  - Seated view: nothing flies outside about 100 degrees (see above).
  - Final turn: fast, with acceleration and deceleration, two full turns.
  - Parts exist in the dark during the spark's flight, seen only by its light,
    and it pushes some of them away.
  - The first build's parts emerge from the dark behind the core, not in from
    outside the view. (Applied to the starter-car build too.)
  - Building is fast, about 180 BPM.
  - The one-wheel car that only sat on the track looked dumb and did not move:
    removed. The nonsense stack is now the FIRST car; the second is a two-wheel
    car like the starter car but with both wheels on one side. The starter car
    in the building area stays as the level makes it.
  - No force when a car fails: let it stop. (The first version threw the
    dropped parts sideways off the track and froze each car on a timer.)
  - The blue rings of the attraction are replaced by a space distortion that
    looks like a black hole, done as a post-process (see THE PULL).
  - The black hole was too large: sized to three circles the user drew on a
    still (see SIZE under THE PULL).
  - Its animation must keep playing until it disappears (see MOTION), and the
    black area beside the player was still too big: sized to a second red
    circle the user drew (see SIZE).

Lessons already paid for:
  - A car whose single wheel lay flat under its nose simply sat at the start.
    The user found it dumb; a failing car has to move.
  - A car with wheels on one side only does not stall: it reaches about
    2.3 m/s, drifts toward its wheel-less side and leaves the 0.8 m bridge.
  - Copy a part's looks from AssemblyPart.VisualRenderers only. Outline helpers
    ("Stencil silhouette") are added under the renderers later and must not be
    copied.
  - The space lens keeps the core and landed parts solid with a stencil mask
    drawn WITHOUT a depth test. With one, the core came out striped in the last
    build: the building area's see-through guides write depth in front of it.
  - The steering rim (found through the intro's final turn, fixed 2026-11-15; not
    an intro bug). Level 1's layout has the rim's visible disc moved 6.6 cm
    forward and 4.8 cm down from its wheel, to sit under the core, and its grid
    pad 6.4 cm behind the core. So the grab zones were not where the rim is
    drawn (it could not be grabbed), and the pad, turning about its own middle,
    swung the rim out from under the core whenever the build turned. Now
    DownhillRotationWheel moves the wheel to its rim on Awake, and
    DownhillPresentation.FollowCore carries the pad around the core. The level
    data was left as it is. The intro tests check the rim after every run.
    Later the same evening another session re-saved Level 1 with the rim's
    offset removed and the pad moved, which left the rim 2.9 cm off the core
    again. FollowCore now also puts the wheel straight under the core every
    frame: a layout decides the rim's height, never where it is centred.
  - Stills taken by the full-run test at 8x can show a thin polygon ring across
    the first pull. It is the core-birth ring, which runs on real time and so
    outlives its beat only at that speed.
  - The title is placed in the world just above the car, facing the player. Head-
    locked at 1.2 m it sat behind the car the player is looking down at and was
    half hidden.
  - The level's clouds and horizon use custom shaders. The track dimmer knows
    their colour names (_Tint, _ShadowTint, _SunTint) and the horizon's
    _Brightness; a new custom shader in a level needs the same treatment or it
    will pop in at full brightness. The water's sparkle pattern has no property
    and stays faintly visible while the track is dark.

REVIEWING
  Editor     Run the play-mode tests in SandboxIntroPlaybackTests. The full-run
             test writes stills of each beat to ArtSource/SandboxIntro/
             CoreIntro-*.png (the intro clock runs 8x there; rings from one beat
             can linger into the next still, which does not happen at normal
             speed). Or enter Play mode in the campaign's Level 1 and use
             MetaHackthone > Cinematic > Play Intro Now.
  Video      Docs/SandboxIntro-playthrough.mp4 is a silent 720p recording of the
             whole intro from the desktop review camera. The file was last
             recorded at 22:00 on 2026-11-15 (43 s, 1249 frames, encoded at
             29 frames a second because the recording ran at 29.24), with the
             swapped cars, the black-hole pull at its final sizes and the
             steering-rim fix. To make a new one, run
             the play-mode test RecordPlaythroughForReview by name (it is
             Explicit, so it never runs with the suite). It plays the intro at a
             real 30 frames a second and writes every frame to
             %TEMP%/MetaHackthoneIntroRecording, logging the exact rate. Encode
             them with Blender's built-in ffmpeg:
               blender -b --factory-startup --python Docs/SandboxIntroEncodeVideo.py
                 -- <frames folder> <output.mp4> 30
             Do not use Time.captureFramerate for this: the intro runs on
             unscaled time, which keeps real time, so the physics runs would
             play at a different speed from everything else. Blender must be set
             to the Standard view transform (the script does this) or the video
             comes out washed.
  Headset    Build with MetaHackthone > Build Quest APK, install, and clear the
             app's data so the seen key is gone. Clearing data also wipes saved
             campaign progress on the headset.

NOT VERIFIED WITHOUT A HEADSET
The space lens has only been run in the editor (one eye, no passthrough). On
the Quest check three things during a pull: that the room is still visible
around the disc (the camera renders through an intermediate texture while the
lens is open, and the frame's alpha has to survive that for passthrough to
show); that both eyes agree; and the frame rate (a full-frame copy per eye).
If the room goes black or the cost is too high, set SandboxSpaceLens.BendFrame
to false: it then skips the copy and the intermediate texture and draws its
"Plain" pass, the same well, ring and spiral blended over the frame with no
bending.
How dark 12 % passthrough looks, the fog's look and fill-rate cost, whether the
cars read at 7 m, spatial sound levels, comfort of the orb's path, and the wait
for the level to be aligned to the head before the intro locks input.
